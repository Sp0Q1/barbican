How scoping works

A pentest is a box

It has a height, a width and a depth. Height is how much you put in it. Depth is how much of the machinery you let us see. Width — the time — is not something you pick first; it follows from the other two.

Depth — how much we get to see
Height — how much goes in

Painted shut. You give us a domain and permission, nothing more. You learn exactly what a stranger can reach — and the first days go to reconnaissance, because that is the point.

Frosted glass. Credentials for each role and an architecture sketch. Usually the sweet spot: we skip the guessing and spend the time on your authorisation logic instead.

Clear glass. Source, pipelines, cloud config and an engineer to answer questions. Finds the flaws no amount of poking from outside will surface — every hour buys depth rather than discovery.

Height
What goes in the box — and what must stay out. Agreed in writing before anything starts.
Depth
How much you hand us up front. More access finds more; less access is more realistic.
Width
Time. Derived, not chosen — we tell you what the box needs, and what a shorter one costs you.

The deliverable

Included on every engagement

AI-augmented,

Machines are extraordinary at reading an entire codebase and never getting bored. They are terrible at knowing which bug matters to your business. We use each for what it is good at, and a human puts their name on the report either way.

What the AI layer does

  • Reads every route, handler and IaC file in scope — not the sample a human would have time for.
  • Maps the authorisation matrix across roles and flags the combinations nobody documented.
  • Drafts and mutates payloads against candidate injection points, then triages the noise.
  • Replays the whole finding set against each new build, so a regressed fix gets caught.
  • Writes the first draft of the reproduction steps. The tester rewrites what is wrong.

What it never does

  • Exploit anything without a named human approving the action against the signed scope.
  • Touch production data, or any system outside the agreed rules of engagement.
  • Put a finding in your report unverified — every one is reproduced by hand first.
  • Reach a model outside the EU without your prior written approval.
  • Replace the tester. It buys back the days they would have spent on grep.

On where your data goes. The platform is EU-hosted. Some frontier testing models are not yet available in the EU, so those are opt-in and named in your engagement letter — never a default, never without written approval. By default everything runs in an isolated tenant that is wiped at closeout.
